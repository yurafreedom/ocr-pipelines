Construction & trades

contractors · estimators · quantity surveyors

Typical pictureEstimators rebuild rates from past quotes by hand, and the clause you need lives on page 34 of a 40-page subcontract.

What it costs you

  • Quotes and BOQs (bills of quantities) are tables. Push them through a plain text extractor and the numbers separate from their line items — ask what you paid for concrete on a job and you get a confident wrong answer.
  • A wrong rate on one line is a mispriced bid: you lose the margin on the whole job, not on the line.
  • “Source: subcontract agreement” isn’t an answer when a delivery dispute needs the notice clause today.

What I buildStructured quote and BOQ extraction that keeps item, quantity, unit, rate and total on the same row; pricing history across past quotes; every answer linked to the exact page and passage it came from.

What changesRates from past quotes in minutes, each one traceable to its page. Contract questions answered with the clause, not the filename.

A few practical questions.

Why not Docparser, Nanonets, Rossum or Hubdoc?

Use them if your documents are clean, single-page invoices with stable layouts and you don’t need to match them against your own orders. They break in three places: tables (rows come apart), evidence (you get a value, not the passage it came from) and matching against your PO, stock or ledger. They also charge per document for as long as you use them. I build the part that sits after capture — checks, matching, review, export — and you own it.

Our documents are too different.

That is what the audit is for. I map document families, exceptions and business rules on your real documents before proposing anything. The first version covers the family with the most volume, not everything at once.

We have phone photos and handwriting.

Photos are straightened and cleaned (deskew, contrast, perspective) before OCR. Handwritten quantities and signatures are the fields most often held for review, so they get a human eye rather than a guess. Expect more review on handwriting — not wrong data in your system.

What accuracy should I expect?

Accuracy differs by field type and scan quality, so I don’t promise a percentage before seeing your documents. Step one is a measured run on 100–200 of yours: how many fields verify automatically, how many go to review, and — the number that matters — how many wrong values reach your system. The review gate keeps that last number at zero by design.

How long does it take?

Audit: 30 minutes. Written scope: within 3 working days. Focused MVP: 2–4 weeks. Production build: 6–12 weeks, depending mostly on integrations.

What happens when a supplier changes their layout?

Extraction is layout-aware, not template-bound, so small changes pass. A genuinely new layout fails its checks and lands in review instead of exporting wrong data, and the corrections made there are used to adjust the system. Under a support retainer I handle new layouts as they appear.

Who fixes it in six months?

You can: the handover includes deployment docs, a test checklist and the code in your repository. If you’d rather not, a monthly retainer covers monitoring, layout changes and small extensions. It is optional and cancellable.

Our documents cannot leave our environment.

Then they don’t. OCR runs on open-source engines on a server you control; where a language model is used, it can be an open-weights model on the same server. Cloud models are used only when you allow it, on your own account.

We don’t want to change our accounting or warehouse system.

You won’t. The system feeds Xero, QuickBooks, Sage or your WMS through their APIs, or through a reviewed CSV import where that is safer. Your team keeps working where they already work.

Where are you based and how do we work together?

Kyiv, two hours ahead of London. A weekly call, a shared Telegram or Slack channel, and a working demo every week rather than a status deck. I sign your NDA or mine.
